## 2. Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs)

Four recurring buyer roles show up across manufacturing-marketing research (fact, aggregated across sources below). Treat these as starting personas — sharpen with real customer titles once available.

### ICP 1 — Design / Mechanical Engineer
- Specifies parts by material, tolerance, and process; often names the fabrication method before procurement gets involved.
- Wants proof of capability before trusting a shop with a design: tolerances held, materials/finishes offered, DFM (design-for-manufacturing) feedback.
- Persuaded by: process detail, real part photos/video, capability specs — not sales copy. (inference, from Design Engineer persona pattern) [ThomasNet](https://blog.thomasnet.com/persona-targeting-manufacturing), [FrictionlessHQ](https://frictionlesshq.com/buyer-persona/procurement-manager-of-manufacturing-persona/)

### ICP 2 — Procurement / Sourcing Manager
- Owns final vendor selection for commodity and custom-manufactured parts; typically 7+ years in procurement/supply chain (fact). [4 Corner Resources](https://www.4cornerresources.com/job-descriptions/procurement-engineer/)
- Optimizes for reliability, on-time delivery, and total cost — not just unit price.
- Persuaded by: consistency (on-time %, quality track record), responsiveness, case studies from comparable industries. (inference)

### ICP 3 — Procurement / Manufacturing Engineer
- The technical-sourcing hybrid: negotiates with suppliers but also evaluates technical fit, cost, and delivery performance against engineering specs (fact). [4 Corner Resources](https://www.4cornerresources.com/job-descriptions/procurement-engineer/)
- Often the first technical filter before a design engineer's spec reaches a procurement manager's sign-off.

### ICP 4 — OEM / Product Manager at a manufacturer that outsources fabrication
- Owns a finished product that includes fabricated sheet-metal components (enclosures, brackets, chassis, HVAC parts, etc.).
- Cares about supply-chain resilience and single-source risk — a second/backup vendor pitch resonates here specifically. (opinion, common OEM pain point)

## 3. Competitive landscape

Two distinct competitor types — don't market against both the same way:

**On-demand manufacturing marketplaces / networks** (fact — named directly in comparison content):
- **Xometry** — largest network (5,000+ partners), broad process coverage, wins on scale and ease of instant quoting. [Jiga](https://jiga.io/articles/xometry-competitors/)
- **Protolabs** — owns its own equipment, heavy automation, wins on speed. [RapidDirect](https://www.rapiddirect.com/blog/top-13-xometry-competitors/)
- **Fictiv** — "managed" network model (~300+ manufacturers), focused on rapid prototyping and low/mid-volume runs. [RapidDirect](https://www.rapiddirect.com/blog/best-fictiv-alternatives/)
- **SendCutSend** — sheet-metal-specific: laser cutting, CNC routing, bending, positioned as a direct sheet-metal alternative rather than a general marketplace. [engineeringunscripted.com](https://engineeringunscripted.com/)
- Others active in the same comparison set: Geomiq, RapidDirect, Haizol, MakerVerse, Shapeways, Unionfab, Quickparts.

**Independent/local job shops** — a real competitor category for most custom sheet metal businesses, but not covered by these sources (inference — this business almost certainly also competes with regional shops, not just national platforms). **[NEEDS INPUT]** name 2-4 actual local/regional competitors.

**Positioning angle:** marketplaces win on speed-of-quote and self-serve scale; they structurally can't offer the relationship, DFM conversation, or shop-floor trust a direct fabricator can. That contrast is a usable content angle, not just a competitive fact. (opinion)

## 4. Platforms this business should work on

- **LinkedIn — primary, by a wide margin.** Generates ~80% of manufacturers' social leads; 93% of manufacturing marketers call it their most effective channel; 85% say it delivers their best social value. B2B discovery on LinkedIn (26.9%) already outpaces YouTube (17.9%) as a search-like starting point. (fact) [Sonnhalter](https://www.sonnhalter.com/2026/06/15/linkedin-best-practices-for-b2b-and-industrial-marketing-in-2026/), [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/manufacturing-social-media-marketing-statistics), [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/social-media-marketing/trends/state-of-linkedin-b2b-marketing)
- **YouTube — secondary, for process/educational depth.** 71% of B2B manufacturing buyers watch video before requesting a quote; YouTube is the best home for longer factory-tour/process-explainer content that's too long for a LinkedIn feed post. (fact) [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/manufacturing-social-media-marketing-statistics)
- **Instagram — narrow, culture/employer-branding use only.** Good for visibility and shop-floor personality (Reels, Stories); explicitly not effective for explaining complex B2B problems or influencing a multi-person buying committee. (fact) [Inrō](https://www.inro.social/blog/linkedin-vs-instagram-outreach-strategy)
- **ThomasNet / industry directories** — not a social platform, but a recurring discovery channel named alongside these buyer personas; worth a presence for inbound RFQ discovery even if it isn't a content-publishing platform. (inference)
- Budget/effort allocation pattern seen across B2B in general: LinkedIn gets 60-70% of organic effort and 80%+ of paid, YouTube and others play a supporting role. (fact, general B2B, not manufacturing-specific) [Improvado](https://improvado.io/blog/social-media-benchmarking)

**Recommendation for this business:** LinkedIn as the primary channel this agent's skills already target — no change needed there. Treat YouTube as a stretch target once there's a backlog of process footage; skip Instagram unless there's a specific hiring/culture goal.

## 5. What kind of posts do well (LinkedIn, manufacturing specifically)

- **Video outperforms static posts** — product demos, factory tours, process explainers, and customer testimonials are the four content types manufacturers see the most engagement from. Video drives ~2.8% engagement, roughly double the baseline for other formats. (fact) [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/manufacturing-social-media-marketing-statistics)
- **Authenticity beats production value.** Short, unpolished shop-floor video reportedly outperforms glossy corporate video — expertise and realness matter more than a slick edit. (fact, stated as a 2026 trend) [Sonnhalter](https://www.sonnhalter.com/2026/06/15/linkedin-best-practices-for-b2b-and-industrial-marketing-in-2026/)
- **Employee/founder-led posts outperform brand-page posts.** Content shared by employees or leadership tends to out-engage the same content posted from the company page. (fact) [Sonnhalter](https://www.sonnhalter.com/2026/06/15/linkedin-best-practices-for-b2b-and-industrial-marketing-in-2026/)
- **Formats that work beyond video:** document/carousel posts (e.g. "5 tolerances every design engineer should know"), first-hand commentary/opinion posts, and interview-style posts all perform well when they carry real expertise rather than generic tips. (fact) [Writeful](https://writefulcopy.com/blog/linkedin-stats-handy-reference-b2b-marketers)
- **What this means for this agent's post types**, mapped onto the four ICPs above (opinion, this session's synthesis):
  1. **Capability/process posts** ("here's how we hold a ±0.1mm tolerance on X") → speaks to Design/Manufacturing Engineers.
  2. **Reliability/track-record posts** (on-time %, a hard delivery story) → speaks to Procurement Managers.
  3. **Case study / before-after posts** (a real customer problem → part → outcome) → speaks to OEM Product Managers, doubles as social proof for everyone else.
  4. **Shop-floor / people posts** (who's running the brake press, a new machine, a certification earned) → builds the trust marketplaces structurally can't replicate — this is the direct-fabricator differentiation angle from section 3.
- The image skill's "deadpan streetwear poster" house style is illustration-first, which is a deliberate contrast to typical manufacturing content (real photos/video) — good for thought-leadership/opinion posts, but real shop-floor photo/video should be a separate, non-illustrated post type once available. **[NEEDS INPUT]** confirm whether real photo/video posts are in scope for this agent, or if it stays text + illustrated-poster only.
