# Research Brief

**Time Window:** 2026-07-26 to 2026-08-25, with two earlier July sources included for strategic and qualification context  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-25

## Summary

The available evidence does not quantify how many OEMs are adding second sheet metal vendors in 2026, but it clearly supports the business case. Manufacturing demand and backlogs are growing while supplier deliveries are slowing, metal input prices remain elevated, and U.S. tariff coverage continues to change. For OEM and product managers, a qualified second fabricator is therefore less about replacing the incumbent or chasing the lowest quote and more about creating ready-to-use capacity, geographic flexibility, cost benchmarks, and continuity if the primary supplier cannot deliver. The practical model is to qualify the second vendor before disruption occurs, then use it for selected parts, regional production, or overflow capacity.

## Key Points

- **(fact)** ISM’s July Manufacturing PMI reached 55.6, its highest reading since May 2022. Supplier deliveries slowed for the eighth consecutive month, the backlog index rose to 55.0, and fabricated metal products reported slower deliveries, higher backlogs, and increasing input prices. ([ismworld.org](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/july/))
- **(fact)** ISM explicitly attributed continuing input-price pressure partly to steel and aluminum prices and tariffs. Its Prices Index registered 71.1 in July, marking the 22nd consecutive month of increasing raw-material prices. ([ismworld.org](https://www.ismworld.org/supply-management-news-and-reports/reports/ism-pmi-reports/pmi/july/))
- **(fact)** BLS reported that steel mill product prices were 22.5% higher year over year and rose 3.9% in July. Fabricated structural metal product prices were 6.2% higher year over year. ([bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ppi.htm))
- **(fact)** On August 6, the Department of Commerce published a proposal to add 14 derivative product categories to Section 232 steel, aluminum, and copper tariffs. Most proposed additions would face a 25% tariff, with some categories subject to 15% or 50% rates. ([public-inspection.federalregister.gov](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-15961.pdf?1785847517=))
- **(fact)** In a July survey of 425 global supply-chain professionals, 71% said balancing cost and risk now drives procurement strategy, but only 45% considered their organizations prepared for disruption. The resulting operating-model recommendations included diversifying supply sources. ([prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ism-and-amazon-business-research-finds-most-organizations-unprepared-for-supply-chain-disruption-despite-strategic-shift-302825549.html))
- **(inference)** For OEMs, these conditions make single-sourcing a product-continuity risk even when the incumbent is performing well. A qualified second sheet metal vendor creates an executable backup for capacity, material availability, freight disruption, tariff exposure, and regional production rather than a contingency that still requires weeks of approval work.
- **(opinion)** The strongest second-source strategy is usually not an immediate 50/50 volume split. Qualify the vendor through first article and pilot production, keep the source active with selected work, and release overflow when the primary supplier’s lead time, capacity, geography, or pricing creates measurable risk.

## Counterpoint

A second vendor is not free insurance. The OEM must maintain revision-controlled drawings, material and finish specifications, quality documentation, first-article approval, pilot production, and potentially duplicate tooling or fixtures. A sheet metal supplier estimates that conventional qualification can take approximately two to six weeks from purchase order to approved first article, plus a pilot run; this is a vendor-provided estimate rather than an independent industry benchmark. Splitting limited volume can also reduce purchasing leverage and create variation between suppliers. If the incumbent has demonstrated capacity, short lead times, strong financial health, regional proximity, and a credible continuity plan, qualifying another vendor may add cost and complexity without enough benefit. In that case, an approved overflow source—rather than a permanent volume split—may be the better model. ([nevatronix.com](https://nevatronix.com/blog/how-oems-qualify-a-second-source-metal-fabricator/))
