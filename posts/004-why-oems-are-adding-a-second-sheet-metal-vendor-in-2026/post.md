## Post
A second sheet metal vendor isn’t a vote against your incumbent.

In 2026, the case is less about chasing a lower quote and more about having approved capacity ready before it’s needed.

July’s Manufacturing PMI reached 55.6, its highest level since May 2022. Supplier deliveries slowed for the eighth consecutive month, while backlogs and metal input prices continued to rise.

Steel mill product prices were also 22.5% higher year over year. Meanwhile, proposed changes to Section 232 tariffs could extend duties to additional derivative product categories.

For OEMs, that combination can make single-sourcing a product-continuity risk—even when the primary supplier is performing well.

The practical alternative usually isn’t an immediate 50/50 volume split. It’s qualifying an overflow source that can support:

- Selected parts or pilot production
- Regional manufacturing needs
- Capacity or lead-time constraints
- Cost and tariff-exposure benchmarks

That preparation still requires revision-controlled drawings, clear material and finish specifications, quality documentation, first-article approval, and pilot production. It can also introduce duplicate tooling, supplier variation, and reduced purchasing leverage.

The takeaway: qualify a second source before disruption creates urgency, then keep it active with carefully selected work.

Which parts in your portfolio would you choose first to validate an overflow sheet metal supplier—and why?
