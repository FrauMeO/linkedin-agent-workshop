# Research Brief

**Time Window:** Evergreen topic; priority given to sources published or updated in 2025  
**Last Updated:** 2026-08-25

## Summary

For procurement and sourcing managers, keeping one qualified manufacturing partner from prototype through the first production run can reduce transition friction: fewer supplier handoffs, less repeated onboarding, retained design-for-manufacturability knowledge, and clearer accountability for quality and delivery. Supplier-quality guidance supports evaluating total cost, technical support, capacity, quality systems, and switching costs—not unit price alone. However, continuity should not become unmanaged dependency: the vendor must be qualified for the intended production process and volume, while critical parts may still require a backup-source strategy.

## Key Points

- **(fact)** ASQ states that supplier-quality management should begin during product design and supplier selection and continue across the product lifecycle. Its supplier criteria include capacity, quality systems, technical support, total cost, communication, incoming verification, and the cost of switching suppliers—not merely purchase price. ([asq.org](https://asq.org/quality-resources/supplier-quality))
- **(fact)** Late-stage prototypes may need to use the same materials and manufacturing processes as production parts. A supplier that supports both stages can apply manufacturability learning before tooling and volume commitments are finalized. ([protolabs.com](https://www.protolabs.com/en-gb/resources/guides-and-trend-reports/prototype-to-production/))
- **(inference)** Staying with a capable vendor preserves context accumulated during quoting, DFM reviews, revisions, validation, tooling, and quality planning. This can reduce the re-explanation, requalification, and interpretation risks introduced by a production handoff.
- **(fact)** In Xometry’s SentriLock case study, one partner supported redesign, additive prototypes, urethane-cast bridge production, and the ramp to full-scale tooling. The customer reported that a single point of contact replaced coordination with at least six separate vendors. ([xometry.com](https://www.xometry.com/resources/case-studies/sentrilock/))
- **(fact)** NIST recommends moving beyond per-unit cost when selecting suppliers, but it also advises buyers to verify production capacity, quality systems, responsiveness, account fit, and the supplier’s ability to grow with demand. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/rethink-your-supply-chain-strategy-better-outcomes))
- **(opinion)** The strongest procurement message is not “never switch vendors.” It is: choose a prototype supplier that can demonstrate a controlled path to production, so switching becomes a risk-based choice rather than an unavoidable project phase.

## Counterpoint

Using one vendor across prototype and production can concentrate operational risk. A supplier that excels at fast, low-volume prototypes may lack the equipment, process controls, certifications, capacity, cost structure, or geographic resilience required for sustained production. NIST therefore recommends balancing supplier consolidation with redundancy for critical components. The defensible position is to maintain continuity only after confirming production capability, quality documentation, capacity, tooling ownership, pricing at forecast volumes, change-control procedures, and contingency arrangements. ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/document/rethink-your-supply-chain-strategy-better-outcomes))
