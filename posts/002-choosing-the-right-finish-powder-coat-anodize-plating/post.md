## LinkedIn post

Anodizing has a reputation as the toughest finish you can put on aluminum. A real abrasion test says otherwise.

SendCutSend ran wire-wheel, bend, and impact tests on finished parts — not spec sheets. Powder coated steel took 73 seconds of abrasion to expose bare metal. Hard anodized aluminum took 8.3 seconds. Powder coated aluminum took 7.

Powder coating also won corrosion resistance on both metals, and beat zinc-plated steel on bend flex and anodized aluminum on impact resistance.

That doesn't make anodizing the wrong choice — it makes "toughest" the wrong reason to pick it. Anodizing still wins on UV and color stability, and it's the only real option where a coating can't be allowed to chip or flake: sliding surfaces, wear plates, aerospace parts. Powder coating works on steel and aluminum and wins almost everywhere else.

One more thing worth knowing before you spec a finish: it adds thickness. Powder coating adds roughly 4.7 thousandths of an inch. Standard (Type 2) anodizing adds about 1. On a tight-tolerance assembly, that gap can matter more than the finish itself.

Pick the material first, the function second, and "which one sounds premium" last.
